# zumir bug hunt, 2026-11-15

Commit `cf9b7b6`. Hunters: 3 on Fable, one at a time, slices: the R layer
(hunt-1); the compiled side, by direction: what zumir compiles against cc,
what it parses as MIR text, the C API, the interpreter and the poll
(hunt-2); lifecycle and ownership: closures, contexts, finalizers, fork,
threads, allocation failure, unload (hunt-3). Coordinator on Opus 5.5.
Built here: macOS x86-64 (both tiers), and Linux x86-64 in a container
(hunt-3, plain and ASan builds). Unverified: Windows, aarch64 on both
systems, macOS x86-64 W+X refusal, ppc64le, s390x and riscv64.

This was the behaviour pass of the critical review (#54 to #108). Every
reproduced trigger below was re-run by the coordinator against a fresh
install of cf9b7b6 before it was filed, and each fix's regression test
was run against that install and seen to fail. The scripts are in
`2026-11-15-bug-hunt-probes/`.

## Cross-cutting

- **The trap's guarantee was enforced per call site, not per path.**
  Findings 6 and 7 are both MIR running outside any trap: one shim that
  did work before pushing its trap, and every call into interpreted code,
  where MIR still allocated lazily. A rule worth stating: an embedder that
  recovers only around its own calls must make the library do all its
  fallible work at a point it controls (MIR_interp_prepare, D27).
- **Text read beside the compiler, not from it.** Finding 2 is a reader
  that re-derived from the source what the compiler had already decided.
  Candidate rule for `correctness.md`: when a tool already parsed an
  input, read its result (here its preprocessed output), never a second
  hand-written reading of the same input.
- **C semantics are a boundary like a protocol.** Findings 11 to 14 were
  invisible to MIR's own suite and to the c-tests corpus, and fell out of
  a differential against the platform's `cc` in an afternoon. The prelude
  tests now carry that differential for each.

## Held: what was probed and stood

Each hunter's report lists its held probes; in short: `mir_compile()`'s
arithmetic, definite assignment, loops, vectors, constants, names and the
two routes and tiers against R, 2,989 random bodies with no difference;
integer promotions, shifts, division, floats, switch, goto, structs,
bit-fields, unions, static locals, recursion, every D6 function at special
values, and 890 random programs against cc; `mir_function()`'s value
matrix at every integer boundary and 45 malformed texts; the C API's
recorded failures on R's thread and another; fork, gctorture, every
allocation failure of `mir_function()` and `mir_compile()` and most of
`cfunction()`'s, SIGINT and time limits, all with the next call exact.

## Not reached

Windows and aarch64 (no runner here); `cfunction()` under interrupts (by
design not interruptible); vectors longer than `.Machine$integer.max`;
real out-of-memory as opposed to the hook; 16 of the 18 random-program
generator crashes were not reduced one by one, but all 80 saved programs
compile on both tiers after the fix and are now fuzz seeds.

## Not reproduced, and by design

None of the reproduced findings failed to re-run. By design: the
interpreter is not shareable across threads (D10); `isnan` and the other
classification macros are not in the prelude (D6); a consumer's import
takes precedence over libm's (now documented in `zumir.h`).
